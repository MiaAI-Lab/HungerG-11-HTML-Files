Atelier Néroli — Blue Hour · Eau de Parfum No. 5

A quiet house page for an invented Grasse perfume, Eau de Parfum No. 5, called Blue Hour. A faceted glass bottle of pale golden liquid sits in a lavender dusk, and the six notes — bergamot, pink pepper, neroli, jasmine sambac, cedar and musk — are the controls. Hover or focus a note and the hour turns with it. A short memory of the garden after the sun has gone, and a discovery set, stay in the same soft register.

Original prompt

A landing page for an invented Grasse perfume house, Atelier Néroli, presenting one fragrance: Eau de Parfum No. 5, "Blue Hour". The mood is the minute between daylight and dream. Weightless, floral, and slow. The page is pale lavender and warm ivory, with ink #2d2838 and a softer #625a70. Display type is a high-contrast serif (Didot, Bodoni, Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia). Labels are a clean sans. The hero is a single object: a transparent faceted glass bottle drawn in line and gradient, pale golden liquid inside, a lavender glass cap, and a small ivory label reading Atelier Néroli, Blue Hour, Eau de Parfum No. 5. The headline is "Blue Hour", with the line "For the minute when the garden forgets the sun." A small fact line reads "Blended in Grasse · 50 ml" and names neroli, jasmine and cedar. The notes are the interaction. Three groups: top, the first light (bergamot, pink pepper); heart, the soft centre (neroli, jasmine sambac); base, what remains (cedar, musk). Hovering, focusing, or pressing a note names it and shifts the colour and light of the hour. A short story, "The memory", follows the scent from the blue minute after sunset. A discovery set can be opened and closed. A control returns the page to the blue hour. Keep the motion quiet: a slow drift of light, not a spectacle. Respect reduced motion by holding the bottle and the notes still while every note remains readable and usable. No external images, fonts, or libraries. All imagery is inline SVG and CSS.

ELASTIC — Letterforms on Springs

A typography physics lab on a near-black field. The opening specimen, BOUNCE, is a cloud of letterforms tied together with springs. Move through it and the letters shove aside. Click, or press Enter, and the word explodes, then finds its way home. Dots, lines or threads change how the same particles are drawn. Stiffness, damping and the pointer's reach are live, and another word of up to twelve characters rebuilds the field.

↗ Open

Original prompt

Build a single self-contained page called ELASTIC, a typography physics lab. The ground is near-black #111018, with raised panels #17151f and #1d1a26, pale type #f3eef7, magenta #ff2e88 and mint #29e7cd. The idea: a word is no longer static type. Its letterforms are particles connected by springs. Open on the specimen BOUNCE, already alive, with the line "Type is no longer static. Disturb the field, release the tension, and watch it find its way home." Moving the pointer through the field pushes the particles. A control switches that field between repel and attract. Clicking the field, or pressing Enter, explodes the word. The springs then pull it back toward the letterforms. Pause freezes the simulation. Reset restores it. Three render modes draw the same particles: Dots, Lines and Threads, chosen with buttons or keys 1–3. A control room sets stiffness (opening at 65), damping (opening at 0.91) and force radius (opening at 150 px). A word field accepts A–Z and 0–9, up to 12 characters, and rebuilds the letterforms when the visitor types, or immediately on Enter. Live readouts show pointer position, point count, energy and frame rate. A keyboard help dialog explains the controls. The page must work by mouse, touch and keyboard, with visible focus. Reduced motion should calm the idle drift without removing the explode, the modes or the typed word. No external assets.

JUMPGATE — Hyperspace Transit

A dark, high-contrast science-fiction jump sequence where star streaks stretch, a glowing wireframe tunnel rushes past and the horizon blooms white as the pilot holds the throttle, framed by a restrained monospace flight HUD.

↗ Open

Original prompt

Build a cinematic, full-viewport hyperspace sequence on a single HTML canvas with a monospace HUD overlay. The palette is pure black (#000000) with electric blue (#3d8bff), pale ice blue (#7fc4ff), near-white (#f4f8ff) and a violet accent (#8a5cff). Letterbox bars of about 6vh sit top and bottom, a radial vignette darkens the edges, and a faint 1px scanline pattern in overlay blend mode gives the frame a projected-film texture. Stars: between 350 and 1400 points (scaled to viewport area) live in a 3D field with random x/y spread and z from 200 to 3000. Each frame they advance toward the camera by a z-speed proportional to the square of the normalised speed; they are projected with a 420px focal length and drawn as round-capped lines from their current position to a point further back along the same ray whose distance grows with speed, so streak length is proportional to velocity. About 12% of stars are violet, 55% blue, the rest white. The canvas is not cleared but faded with a translucent black rectangle whose opacity decreases as speed rises, leaving longer luminous trails. All drawing uses the "lighter" composite mode over a radial core glow at the vanishing point that swells from a faint 40px disc to a blinding white-blue bloom at maximum speed. Tunnel: 14 octagonal wireframe rings spaced along z recede toward the horizon, each slowly spinning (faster at speed), stroked in blue with a matching shadowBlur glow, with violet struts connecting every other ring to its neighbour. When a ring passes the camera it wraps to the far end. Interaction: holding the mouse button, a touch, or the spacebar sets the target speed to 1 and the speed eases toward it with an exponential approach (faster ramp up than decay); releasing lets it coast down. Above 75% speed a random per-frame screen shake grows to 6px and a chromatic split appears, drawing red- and blue-offset copies of each streak that separate by up to 6px scaled by projection depth. Crossing 93% triggers a brief white flash; the speed readout gains a white glow at 97%+. HUD: system-ui/ui-monospace, light-blue text with a soft blue text-shadow. Corners show vector, gate id, hull integrity (drops slightly at max), drive state (IDLE / BURN / COAST / MAX), live frame rate, star count, distance travelled in light-years, elapsed time and shake magnitude. Centered at the bottom is the big thin-weight speed number in "c" units (0.00–9.99) with a gradient progress bar and a stage label (sublight / warp entry / hyperspace / threshold). A centred SVG reticle has two counter-rotating dashed rings (14s and 22s), tick marks and a pulsing center dot. The title "JUMPGATE" in 0.5em-tracked ultralight uppercase sits at the top with a prompt line that fades once engaged. Reduced-motion users get no shake and static reticle rings.

NGC 7741 Meridian — Observatory

A full-screen additive-blended particle galaxy with two logarithmic spiral arms, warm core, blue star-forming knots and red outer dust, rotating differentially beneath a restrained observatory HUD whose statistics count up on load. The viewer tilts and spins the disk by dragging, zooms by scrolling, and pauses with the spacebar.

↗ Open

Original prompt

Create a single self-contained HTML page that renders a rotating spiral galaxy made of tens of thousands of particles on a full-viewport 2D canvas, presented as a quiet astronomical observatory instrument. The page is pure black (#000000) with a very faint starfield and a dozen distant, softly blurred background galaxies (tilted ellipses drawn as radial gradients) pre-rendered to an offscreen canvas. A radial vignette darkens the corners. The galaxy itself is built from roughly 42,000 points (drop to ~14,000 when prefers-reduced-motion is set). Eighteen percent form a spherical bulge using Gaussian scatter; the remainder are distributed along two logarithmic spiral arms using r = a·e^(bθ) with b ≈ 0.28, an exponential density falloff in radius (mean 0.32 of the disk radius), and angular scatter that widens with radius so arms are crisp near the core and diffuse at the rim. Each particle stores radius, base angle, vertical height (disk thickness ~0.06 scaled by radius), size and brightness. Colors are mapped by radius: warm white/yellow in the core (#FFEBBE to #FFCD82), blue-white along the arms (#CDD7FF to #91C3FF), and faint red-magenta dust at the outer edge (#AA7882 to #D25058). About six percent of arm particles are "hot" oversized blue knots at full brightness. Rotation is differential: angular velocity ω = 0.35 / (0.25 + r), so the inner disk visibly outruns the outer arms and the spiral winds over time. Project the 3D disk to 2D with a yaw rotation about the disk normal followed by a tilt rotation about the screen X axis; depth slightly scales point size. Render with globalCompositeOperation "lighter" for additive glow, drawing every particle as a pre-rendered 32px radial-gradient sprite tinted per quantized color and cached in a Map. Above the disk, a "nebula dust" layer of ~90 large soft radial-gradient ellipses follows the arms (warm peach near the core, cobalt and wine-red farther out) at 6–9% alpha, plus a warm radial core glow. HUD typography: designation line in a tiny uppercase monospace with 0.32em tracking (#6D7787), the galaxy name "NGC 7741 Meridian" in a large serif (Iowan Old Style / Palatino / Georgia) with the word Meridian italic in amber (#FFD9A0) and a soft amber text-shadow, and an italic serif classification paragraph. The bottom-left stats strip (Particles, Distance in Mly, Diameter in kly, Tilt, Zoom) uses tabular monospace numerals with the first three counting up over ~3.2 seconds with a quartic ease-out. The HUD fades in over 2.4 seconds. Top-right holds a small SVG compass whose needle rotates with the yaw. Right edge has four round glass buttons (zoom in, zoom out, pause, reset) with blur backdrop, scaling to 1.12 on hover and glowing amber at the border. Interactions: pointer drag changes yaw (horizontal) and tilt (vertical, clamped between near face-on and near edge-on); mouse wheel and two-finger pinch zoom between 0.35× and 6× with eased approach; Space toggles rotation; reset returns to the default oblique view. The canvas must handle window resize and devicePixelRatio (capped at 2). Mood: contemplative, precise, slightly reverent — a planetarium display at midnight.

Murmuration at dusk

A wide marsh sky in the last minutes of light: plum-ink overhead sinking through mauve and coral to a pale apricot horizon, a low sun and its broken reflection in a thin strip of water, pylons and poplars tiny on the far bank, and a black reed bed swaying along the bottom edge. Into the empty sky a long ribbon of starlings streams from the right and gathers into a single breathing shape made of thousands of tiny flapping chevrons, lighter where the birds are far and darker where the flock folds over itself, stretching into ribbons and hourglasses and rippling with sudden dark waves when it turns. The viewer's pointer is a peregrine; the flock parts around it, wraps behind it and closes again, and a click sends the falcon into a tucked dive that tears a hole straight through the birds. Left alone, the falcon hunts by itself, the light slowly fails, and the whole flock drains through a narrow neck into the reeds; after a few seconds of stillness a new evening begins. The only words are a tiny italic title, a live count of starlings, a hint that dissolves once the hunt starts, and two quiet controls.

↗ Open

Original prompt

A full-bleed simulation of a starling murmuration over a reed marsh at dusk, on Canvas 2D. Concept: thousands of simple birds becoming one dark, folding fabric in the sky; the viewer is the falcon. Mood: awe, hush, the last ten minutes of light. Palette: sky as a tall vertical gradient from pale apricot #F6C9A8 at the horizon through coral #E58F7B and mauve #6B4E71 to deep plum-ink #2A2438 at the top; birds, reeds and far treeline in near-black #0D0B12; a low sun disc and its reflection in a thin strip of marsh water in a paler apricot. This dusk gradient is the concept, not a default; keep it painterly with faint horizontal washes, a faint grain and a few long thin horizontal clouds that drift very slowly. Simulation: 2,500–3,600 boids on desktop (scale down automatically on small or slow devices, never below 1,200), using a uniform spatial grid for neighbour lookup, classic separation / alignment / cohesion with topological-ish neighbour limits (nearest ~7 sampled from the surrounding cells), plus a wider neighbourhood term taken from per-cell sums (shared heading, a pull to the local centre of mass, and a soft pressure that pushes birds down the density gradient when a region gets too crowded) so the flock holds together as one fabric without collapsing into a ball. A soft roosting attractor orbits slowly above the reed bed so the flock stays on screen; it periodically splits into two lobes along a rotating axis so the flock stretches into ribbons and hourglasses and then rejoins. Add a little coherent noise turbulence. Add fake depth: each bird has a z value (part its own, part a slow sheet that drifts through the flock) that scales its size (roughly 1–3px strokes) and darkness, and a soft density haze under the flock, so it reads as a volume whose density makes darker folds where it overlaps itself. Draw birds as tiny oriented chevrons (two short strokes that flap by alternating length), not dots. "Waves of agitation": when birds turn sharply they briefly thicken and lengthen their strokes, and an excitable wave passes from bird to neighbour with a short delay and a refractory period, so alarms ripple visibly through the flock; now and then something unseen spooks one edge of it. Interaction: the pointer/finger is a peregrine: a small falcon silhouette glides toward the pointer position with inertia (a faint reticle marks the pointer itself, and the falcon circles it rather than stopping); birds within a radius flee and dodge sideways off its line of flight, so the flock splits, wraps and re-forms around it, the signature moment. A click/tap (or the space bar) makes the falcon stoop: wings tucked, a fast dive toward the pointer, or along its heading if the pointer is already beneath it, which punches a hole through the flock and leaves a thin streak and a faint ring. If the pointer is idle the falcon loiters high and makes occasional autonomous passes, some with a stoop, so the page is alive without input. Over about three minutes the light fades one step (sky darkens ~15%), then the flock gathers above the roost, funnels down through a narrow neck and pours into the reeds like liquid (the reeds shiver where birds land), the screen rests for a few seconds, and a new evening begins; a small control lets the viewer "call them down" or "send them up" at will, the latter erupting the whole flock out of the reed bed. Foreground: a reed bed silhouette along the bottom in two layers with individually swaying stems, drooping plumes, bulrush heads and leaves (sine sway with phase by x), a leaning fence post with sagging wire, two distant pylons with their wires and a clump of poplars on the far treeline for scale, and glints flickering in the sun's reflection. Typography: almost none. Bottom-left in tiny letterspaced Georgia italic, cream at 80%: "Murmuration at dusk" and a live, quietly updating count line "3,200 starlings · one falcon" (it counts birds in view, and reads "3,200 starlings in the reeds · one falcon, hungry" while the flock roosts); a designed hint with a small falcon glyph, "move to hunt · click to stoop" ("drag to hunt · tap to stoop" on touch), that fades after first interaction. Sound toggle bottom-right (off by default): a soft rush of wings via band-passed noise with a fast flutter, whose volume and filter follow the flock's average speed, its presence on screen, its proximity to the falcon and how many birds are fleeing. Entrance: the empty sky, then the flock streams in from the right as a long ribbon and balls up. Performance: typed arrays, batched paths per depth band, the static sky, the drifting clouds, the birds and the reeds on separate stacked layers, DPR capped at 2, pause when hidden, flock thinned automatically if frames run long. Reduced motion: 1,200 birds, everything at well under half speed, no stoop streak or ring and no autonomous attacks; a still composition with a gently drifting flock.

Northern Lights — Lake Inari, 68.9° N

A meditative full-screen canvas landscape in which layered noise-driven curtains of green and violet light drift over a black pine horizon and are mirrored in a still, faintly rippling lake, with twinkling stars, occasional meteors and slow snowfall, framed by a sparse serif caption and a single glass slider that controls how strongly the sky burns.

↗ Open

Original prompt

Build a single self-contained, full-viewport HTML page titled "Northern Lights — Lake Inari, 68.9° N" that renders a calm, realistic aurora borealis night scene entirely on a 2D canvas, with a minimal glass UI floating on top. The mood is hushed and immersive: a near-black indigo sky (#02030a at the zenith fading through #061021 to #0b1a30 at the horizon), a frozen lake occupying the bottom 30 % of the screen, a jagged black pine treeline (#02040a in front, #050a14 for a softer distant row) along the horizon, and curtains of light dominated by aurora green (#3cf5a0, hue ≈150–158) with violet (#a86bff, hue ≈285) and pink (hue ≈330) fringes at the top edges. Text is cool fog white (rgba(226,236,255,0.72)) with mint highlights (#c8ffe4). Aurora construction: render to a low-resolution offscreen canvas (about one third of the screen width) and upscale with smoothing so the light is naturally soft. Define four curtains, each with its own hue, saturation, drift speed and direction, spatial frequency, height and alpha. For every column of the offscreen canvas compute a brightness from a four-octave value-noise fBm sampled along x with a time offset, mixed with a slow horizontal sine sway and a slower "ribbon" sine, raised to a power of 1.8 so bright folds stay rare; compute a bottom edge that undulates around 72 % of the sky height and a top edge that reaches up by the curtain's height scaled by noise and the intensity setting; then fill a 1.6-pixel-wide vertical linear gradient that is transparent at the top, mid-alpha in the middle, brightest just above the bottom edge and transparent again at the base. Composite curtains with 'lighter' onto the offscreen canvas and draw that onto the sky with 'screen'. Add a faint green glow band rising from the horizon. Sky details: a few hundred stars sized by area, most tiny, 8 % larger, 20 % warm-tinted (#ffe7c4) and the rest blue-white (#dfe9ff), each twinkling on its own sine phase and dimming slightly as intensity rises. Occasionally (about once every eight seconds) spawn a shooting star: a short white gradient streak that travels at a shallow angle and fades over roughly a second. Lake: paint a dark water gradient (#08121f → #020409), then reflect the aurora by drawing the offscreen aurora canvas in 4-pixel horizontal slices from the horizon downward, sampling upward from the bottom of the sky image (mirror, slightly compressed by 1.3×), offsetting each slice horizontally with two summed sines that grow with depth to create a wavy distortion, and fading alpha with depth. Draw the treeline mirrored the same sliced way with a weaker wobble so the silhouettes shimmer. Sprinkle a dozen thin, slowly wandering horizontal highlight streaks for ripple glints. Finally draw the treeline itself, generated once by walking across the width and stamping pine shapes made of a trunk plus four stacked triangles whose heights and spacing are driven by 1-D noise. Snow: a light field of small white discs (0.6–2.2 px) falling at 12–38 px/s with a gentle sinusoidal drift, recycled to the top when they leave the screen, toggleable. UI: top-left a monospace eyebrow "Field observation · Kp 5" with a pulsing green dot; top-right the live local clock. Bottom-left a serif caption block (Georgia / Iowan Old Style / Palatino) with the headline "The sky breathing over Lake Inari" (italic mint accent word), a short scientific paragraph explaining oxygen green and high-altitude violet emissions, and a monospace meta row (68.9° N 27.0° E, −23 °C, wind calm, cloud 0/8). Bottom row: a frosted pill (blurred dark glass, hairline mint border, lifts on hover) holding an "Intensity" range slider 0–100 (default 62) with a gradient track from mint through green to violet and a glowing white thumb that springs larger when dragged, the numeric output, and a "Snow" toggle pill with aria-pressed; bottom-right a tiny wavelength legend (Oxygen 557.7 nm / Nitrogen 427.8 nm). The caption fades up on load. A CSS vignette darkens the corners. Handle devicePixelRatio (capped at 2), debounce resize and rebuild stars, snow and treeline; under prefers-reduced-motion render a single static frame and skip the shooting stars and snowfall.

Original prompt

A landing page for an invented small-batch hot sauce brand, "Volcán Chico", designed in a loud, joyful Memphis-Milano manner: squiggles, confetti triangles, terrazzo dots, thick black outlines, hard drop shadows, clashing colour blocks. Deliberately maximal; the opposite of a tasteful dark SaaS page. Palette (exact): lava orange-red #FF4B1F, banana yellow #FFD60A, teal #00A6A6, bubblegum pink #FF7BAC, ink black #111111, cream #FFF8E7. Light, saturated colour-field sections that change colour block by block. Flat colour only: no gradients except on the sauce liquid, no blur.
